Coverage Gaps in Canada’s PM2.5 Monitoring Network and Recommendations for Network Expansion

How can we improve access to smoke monitoring in Canada?

Brayden Nilson

Applied Air Quality Scientist, ECCC (AQ Science Unit - West)

June 11, 2026

Background

PM2.5 monitoring is costly, but access to real-time information is crucial during smoke events

1

2

  • high upfront/maintenance cost
  • a single point, may miss regional variations
  • wildfire smoke is not concentrated in urban areas like many other sources

  1. Cold Lake, AB monitor. LICA (2026). https://lica.ca/airshed/regional-air-monitoring-programs/

  2. 2023 Wildfires PM2.5 Maxima. Jain et al. (2024). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51154-7

Low-cost monitors (PurpleAir, AQegg, etc) are a cost effective means to supplement monitoring

1

  • orders of magnitude cheaper to purchase (~$50k -> < $500)
  • able to deploy without dedicated infrastructure
  • accurate enough for detecting smoke events when bias-corrected2

  1. https://www.purpleair.com/

  2. Nilson et al. (2022). https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-15-3315-2022

It is difficult to determine individual access to monitoring data, but gaps do exist

Input Data

Identified who needs access using community locations and gridded Census population

2016 Census used – lack of 2021 gridded data

Considered existing regulatory (NAPS FEM) and PurpleAir monitors with PM2.5 data

Assumed possible to install a new monitor at any community (not always true!)

Which communities could a monitor be installed in, such that someone without a monitor within 25 km would now be covered?

Need:

  1. A host (i.e. community member, local government, schools, etc)
  2. Wi-Fi (may not be available/reliable, in range, or allowed on some networks)
  3. Power (ex. my home only has one exterior plug, limiting install)
  4. Space (a monitor under a covered patio beside a BBQ is not very useful!)

People, places, and PM2.5 monitors differ across provinces and territories

The existing monitoring networks cover ~92% of Canadians, but only ~59% of communities
